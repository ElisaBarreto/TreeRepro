# RFC-70: Contribution Workflow

## Context
When an expert contributes a claim (a value for a trait of a species), they provide references (DOIs, books by ISBN, or a personal observation) and say how the claim relates to existing data: independent, complementary or contesting. The system creates one record per level from these sources. An entry that repeats an existing record becomes a validation of it instead. A contest states which levels are correct and marks the other existing levels as contested until a reviewer resolves it.

## Rules
- **R1** `POST /api/records` (`records.create`) body: `{ speciesId, traitId, value, sources, intent?, respondsToRecordId?, contestedLevelIds?, rawValue?, note?, secondaryReferenceId? }`. `sources` is `{ personalObservation: true }` or `{ references: [...] }`, an array of 1–10 entries, each `{ id }`, `{ doi }` or `{ isbn, citation }` (RFC-61 R10), distinct after normalisation. `value` is RFC-65 R1. `contestedLevelIds` is an array of at most 100 distinct level ids. Once the trait is known (RFC-65 R1): without `intent`, neither `respondsToRecordId` nor `contestedLevelIds` is given; a `complement`, and a `contest` on a quantitative trait, carry `respondsToRecordId` and no `contestedLevelIds`; a `contest` on a categorical trait carries `contestedLevelIds` (the levels it contests, R3) and no `respondsToRecordId`. Any other combination answers 400 `VALIDATION_FAILED` with path `intent`. Visibility (RFC-33 R5) applies to the species, the trait and the responded record.
- **R2** Resolution: first the species, trait, value and level checks of RFC-65 R1. Then `responds_to`, when given: the record must exist and be visible (404 `RECORD_NOT_FOUND`; a withdrawn record is not visible, RFC-63 R13) and belong to the species and trait (400, path `respondsToRecordId`). A `contest` (RFC-63 R14) always carries a value. A quantitative contest's value must differ from the responded record's in at least one of the six fields (400 `VALIDATION_FAILED`, path `value`, "A contest carries a different value"); a categorical contest's levels are checked in R3. A complement may repeat the value under another reference. Then the sources resolve to reference ids: RFC-80 R5 for a DOI, RFC-61 R10 for an ISBN, and RFC-61 R7 for a personal observation. A reference named by `id` must not be a personal observation: naming one answers 409 `REFERENCE_IS_PERSONAL` (RFC-61 R7).
- **R3** Insert, in one transaction. A form creates one record per level (categorical) or one record (quantitative). Every record carries all the resolved references — the first as `primary_reference_id`, the rest as `record_references` rows (RFC-63 R16) — and otherwise identical columns (`origin = 'manual'`, `harmonisation = 'harmonised'`, `created_by = actor`, `intent`, `responds_to_record_id`, `raw_value`, `note`, `secondary_reference_id`). Before inserting, each entry — a level, or the quantitative value — is matched against the visible records of the species and trait. A categorical entry matches the records of the same level; a quantitative one matches a record whose six fields are all identical. A matched entry creates no record, whatever the `intent`. Each matching record the actor did not create gets the actor's references as validations — one `confirm` per reference, or a single `confirm` without a reference for a personal observation (RFC-65 R3) — and is reported in `validated`. A matching record that is the actor's own is only reported in `duplicates`. In a form that mixes new and matching levels, the new levels create records and the matching ones become validations. A categorical contest splits three ways, inside the transaction and under the species × trait lock the withdraw paths take: E is the set of active levels with a record of the species and trait visible to the actor (RFC-33 R2) at that moment, S is `value.levelIds`; the levels of S ∩ E are validations (or duplicates) as above, the levels of S \ E create records with `intent = 'contest'` and `responds_to_record_id` null, and the levels of E \ S are the levels it contests (RFC-63 R14). When E \ S is empty the request answers 400 `VALIDATION_FAILED` with path `intent`, "A contest must contest at least one level; this is a complement"; otherwise, when `contestedLevelIds` differs from E \ S as a set, it answers 400 `VALIDATION_FAILED` with path `contestedLevelIds`. Either answer writes no record, annotation or contest. Otherwise the contest is stored with its author, its time and E \ S, whether or not it created a record (how a contest that creates none is stored is plan 13g's). A quantitative contest whose value matches a visible record creates no record and contests nothing: it counts as the actor's validation of the matching record (or as a duplicate when that record is the actor's own), and the web app says so ("matches an existing record — counted as your validation"). An insert that still collides with `trait_records_claim_key` (RFC-63 R3) creates nothing. It is reported in `duplicates` when the colliding record is visible to the actor; otherwise (withdrawn, RFC-63 R13, or not harmonised) nothing names it (RFC-33 R4), so a withdrawn claim cannot be entered again under the same references. Answer 201 `{ created: [record item…], validated: [{ recordId, recordCode }], duplicates: [{ recordId, recordCode }] }` for any mix. No request answers 409 `RECORD_DUPLICATE` (RFC-65 R2, retired). The web app names each `validated` record as "matches an existing record — counted as your validation".
- **R4** `POST /api/records/:id/annotations` (RFC-65 R3): `confirm` and `withdraw` need `records.annotate`; Keep both and Withdraw contest act on a contest (RFC-65 R16). Which record an actor may withdraw is RFC-65 R4: the handler passes the actor's `records.withdraw` and `records.withdraw_imported` to the service, as it passes `canWithdrawAny` today. The annotation representation carries `reference: { id, citationKey, kind } | null` and `generated: boolean` (RFC-63 R8).
- **R5** (retired) Withdrawing a contest no longer writes a `neutral` annotation: the contested flag is derived from standing contests (RFC-63 R14), so withdrawing the contest clears it by itself. Plan 13g removes the code tagged with this rule.
- **R6** Representations (RFC-63 R8): the record item carries `intent: 'contest' | 'complement' | null` and `respondsTo: { id } | null`; the detail adds `responses: [{ id, intent, createdBy, createdAt }]` (visible records naming this one, newest first). The reference ref (`{ id, citationKey }`) gains `kind`: `publication`, `book` or `personal_observation`.
- **R7** `GET /api/species/:id/traits?includeMissing=true` (RFC-63 R10): with the flag, the response also lists every trait visible to the viewer (RFC-33 R2) that has no visible record for the species, as `{ trait, recordCount: 0, harmonisationCounts: all zero, numeric: null, validated: false, contested: false }`, with `levels` following the RFC-63 R10 rule — `[]` for a categorical trait and `null` for a quantitative one, so `levels === null` still means "quantitative" whatever the record count. Traits come in dictionary order, and categories that only have missing traits appear. Without the flag the response is unchanged.
- **R8** Audit: none for records and annotations (RFC-65 R12); reference creation audits per RFC-61 R7–R8.
- **R9** Species page. A legend at the top reads 👍 **Validate** · 👎 **Contest** · ＋ **Complement**. The symbols are the `Icon` stroke glyphs `thumbsUp`, `thumbsDown` and `plus` (`components/ui/Icon.tsx`), never emoji; the emoji in this rule are shorthand. A categorical trait card lists every level the species has (RFC-63 R10, no cap), each with the three buttons. A quantitative trait has them on each row of its record panel (R11). 👍 asks "Do you confirm that this record is correct?", with an optional supporting reference (a DOI, or an ISBN with its citation). It then validates every record of the level (RFC-65 R13), or the one record of a quantitative row (RFC-65 R3). 👎 on a level opens the entry dialog (R10) with **Contest** chosen, that level unchecked and every other level with visible records checked; the user adjusts the levels. 👎 on a quantitative row opens it with **Contest** chosen and that record as the responded record. ＋ opens it with the responded record set (a record of the level, or the row's record), and the user still makes R10's required choice between Contest and Complement. Withdrawing asks for a confirmation only (RFC-65 R4). The buttons render per RFC-13 R3, and the API decides (RFC-65 R3, R4, R13).
- **R10** Entry dialog. When the species already has visible records for the trait, the first step is a required choice between **Contest** and **Complement**. Every other field and the submit button stay disabled until the user answers, whether the dialog opened from a card's ＋ or from the page header. From the header, the user also picks the record a complement or a quantitative contest responds to; a categorical contest responds to no record (R1). Levels are chosen with checkboxes. Choosing **Contest** on a categorical trait checks every active level with visible records, except the level whose 👎 opened the dialog; the user adjusts. Before submit, a categorical entry whose E is not empty shows a confirmation summary of what it will do per level, computed from the trait's active levels with visible records (E) and the checked levels (S): "Validate" the checked levels in E, "Add" the checked levels not in E and, for a contest, "Contest" the levels of E left unchecked — for example "Validate red · Contest blue, yellow · Add green". The submit button stays disabled until the user confirms the summary, and any change to the levels asks for the confirmation again. When E is empty (a first entry on a trait with no records) there is nothing to validate or contest, so no summary is shown. A contest whose summary contests no level cannot be submitted; the dialog says "A contest must contest at least one level; this is a complement". The request sends the summary's contested levels as `contestedLevelIds` (R1). When the API answers 400 with path `contestedLevelIds` (the levels changed meanwhile), the dialog reloads the trait's levels and asks for the confirmation again. A quantitative trait offers six fields: single, min, max, mean, SD and n (RFC-63 R15). Sources accept DOIs, ISBNs with their citation, or neither, which records a personal observation (R1).
- **R11** Record panel. Its columns sort through the server-side `sort` of RFC-63 R9 (value, references, origin, added). A counts column shows ✓ `validationCount` / ✗ `contestCount`, plus a **Contested** badge when `contested` is true (RFC-63 R8). Withdrawn records are not shown (RFC-63 R13).
