# RFC-63 — Trait records and provenance

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | accepted |
| Category | dataset |
| Supersedes | — |

## Context

A trait record is one claim: a reference reports that a species has a trait with a value. Sources disagree, harmonisation fails for some rows, and scientists will confirm, dispute and add claims. The owner's requirements: the original imported value is marked as such and never lost; every addition and every curation step is traceable, including intermediate states. Records are therefore insert-only; a correction is a contest stating the correct value (R14), never an edit. The platform stores every claim and never picks one (R11).

## Rules

- **R1** A trait record has `record_code`, `intent`, `responds_to_record_id`, `species_id`, `trait_id`, `level_id`, `numeric_value`, `min_value`, `max_value`, `mean_value`, `median_value`, `sd_value`, `se_value`, `n`, `value_text`, `harmonisation`, `raw_value`, `original_trait_name`, `original_species_name`, `secondary_source_species_name`, `raw_category`, `primary_reference_id`, `secondary_reference_id`, `origin`, `import_batch_id`, `import_row_no`, `unit_status`, `source_folder`, `source_file`, `taxonomic_status`, `gbif_genus`, `gbif_family`, `taxon_order`, `unit`, `summary_source`, `records_behind_row`, `created_by`, `note`, `supersedes_record_id`, `created_at`. `record_code` is R12; `numeric_value` is the *single* value and `min_value` … `se_value` are R15; `unit_status`, `source_folder` and `source_file` are nullable `text`, and `taxonomic_status` is a nullable `text` naming how the row's species name matched (exact match, resolved synonym, fuzzy match…), and `gbif_genus`, `gbif_family` and `taxon_order` are nullable `text` giving the genus and family the GBIF backbone accepts and the order the source reported — all seven are import provenance, copied from the row's own columns (RFC-64 R2), and the platform's taxonomy stays the one of RFC-60; `unit` is a nullable `text` naming the unit the record's numbers are in, null meaning the trait's standard unit (RFC-62) — the colour traits carry per-record scales that are not interconvertible; `summary_source` is a nullable `text` in `reported_by_study`, `derived_from_records`, `reported_and_derived`, saying whether the record's statistics were reported by its study or computed from the study's measurements (only the mean is ever computed); `records_behind_row` is a nullable `integer >= 1`, how many harmonised source records the imported row stands for (not a sample size, that is `n`) — these three are import provenance as well, none enters `value_text` or the claim key (R3), and a manual record leaves them null; a manual record's further references are rows of `record_references` (R16).
- **R2** Inserts are via `intent`: a check requires `intent` whenever `responds_to_record_id` is set, and `responds_to_record_id` whenever `intent = 'complement'`; a contest record has it for a quantitative trait and never for a categorical one (R14; enforced by RFC-70 R1, not by the database check). A trigger refuses a response to a record of another species or trait. Checks apply per origin and harmonisation. The quantitative columns are checked by the database: a level excludes all eight (single, min, max, mean, median, sd, se, n); any of them implies `harmonised`; `trait_records_quantitative_check` holds `min_value <= max_value`, `sd_value >= 0` and `n >= 1`. `record_references` is insert-only for `treerepro_app` (no `UPDATE`, `DELETE` or `TRUNCATE`).
- **R3** Claim uniqueness: `(species_id, trait_id, value_text, raw_value, primary_reference_id, secondary_reference_id)` is unique with nulls not distinct (constraint `trait_records_claim_key`). Each quantity has its own field (R15) and `value_text` names every field but a lone single value, so a single value, a mean and a median of the same number from the same source are three claims (`5`, `mean=5`, `median=5`). An identical claim is never stored twice; the import counts the collision as a duplicate (RFC-64 R8).
- **R4** Immutability: `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE` never succeed on `trait_records`, `record_annotations`, `contests`, `contest_levels`, `contest_records` and `contest_events`: a trigger raises `insufficient_privilege` and the `treerepro_app` role holds neither `UPDATE` nor `DELETE` on any of them (`0012_dataset_append_only.sql`; the four contest tables get the same triggers). Corrections are new records; retractions are annotations (RFC-65). The sanctioned exceptions are the replacing imports of RFC-64 R12 and R15: each runs as `treerepro_migrator`, disables these triggers inside its transaction and restores them there; R15 alone also deletes and re-inserts, with their ids, the annotations it re-links, and rewrites `intent`, `responds_to_record_id` and `supersedes_record_id` of the `TR_` rows that point at an `EB_` record. They touch `trait_records` and `record_annotations` only — the contest tables have no exception. `treerepro_app` never holds `UPDATE` or `DELETE` on these tables, so the guarantee is unchanged for the application.
- **R5** Harmonisation axis, set when the record is created and never changed: `harmonised` (categorical with a `level_id`, or quantitative with at least one of `numeric_value`, `min_value`, `max_value`, `mean_value` and `median_value`, R15), `unknown_level` (categorical, no level matches), `not_numeric` (quantitative, the value is not a number per RFC-64 R6), `empty` (blank value). `multi_value` remains a valid stored status for records created before this rule, but is never produced: a categorical value containing `;` reports several states and is split into one record per part (RFC-64 R6).
- **R6** Review state, derived when a record is read, one of: `contested` when the record, or the level it carries, is contested (R14); else `validated` when the record has at least one `confirm` annotation (RFC-65 R3); else `unvalidated`. A record with a `withdraw` annotation has left the dataset (R13) and has no review state for any viewer.
- **R7** A record has many `record_annotations` (with `reference_id` and `generated`), written by RFC-65 and read here. The kinds in use are `confirm` (a validation, RFC-65 R3) and `withdraw` (RFC-65 R4). Keep both on a contest (RFC-65 R15, R16) is a `resolve` row of `contest_events`, not a `record_annotations` row; `record_annotations` holds only `confirm` and `withdraw` from now on. `dispute` and `neutral` rows written before plan 13g stay in the table and every rule ignores them.
- **R8** `GET /api/records/:id` The item exposes `recordCode`, `intent`, `respondsTo`, `unitStatus` (R1; null when absent), `unit` (R1; the record's own unit as imported, null when the source gave none), `quantitative: { single, min, max, mean, median, sd, se, n } | null` (R15; null for a categorical record), `references: [reference ref…]` (the primary reference first, then the `record_references` rows of R16), `validationCount` (distinct actors with a `confirm` on the record), `contestCount` (distinct authors of the contests that are not withdrawn — resolved ones included — and name the record's level among the levels they contest, or respond to the record itself for a quantitative trait, R14) and `contested: boolean` (R14). The detail exposes `responses`, `sourceFolder`, `sourceFile`, `taxonomicStatus`, `gbifGenus`, `gbifFamily`, `taxonOrder`, `summarySource` and `recordsBehindRow` (R1; each null when absent). An annotation exposes `reference`, `generated`.
- **R9** `GET /api/records?speciesId=&traitId=&referenceId=&cursor=&limit=` (`dataset.read`): either `speciesId` and `traitId` together, or `referenceId` alone (primary or secondary), a `record_references` row (R16) naming it too; any other combination answers 400 `VALIDATION_FAILED` with detail path `speciesId`. `sort` is `value`, `references`, `origin` or `added` and `order` is `asc` or `desc` (default `added desc`; any other value answers 400 `VALIDATION_FAILED` with path `sort` or `order`): `added` orders by `id`, `value` by the level key (categorical) or by `coalesce(numeric_value, mean_value, median_value, min_value, max_value)` (quantitative), `references` by the primary reference's `short_citation` then `citation_key`, `origin` by `origin`, each with `id` as the tiebreaker, under the composite keyset cursor `[sort key, id]` (RFC-11 R6). Only visible records are listed (RFC-33 R2). `GET /api/records/:id` returns the detail; unknown id answers 404 `RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.
- **R10** `GET /api/species/:id/traits` supports `missingOnly` (RFC-70 R7). It answers `[{ category: { key, label }, traits: [{ trait, recordCount, harmonisationCounts: { harmonised, unknownLevel, multiValue, notNumeric, empty }, levels: [{ levelId, key, count, validationCount, contested }] | null, numeric: [{ unit, means: { single, mean, median, min, max }, count }] | null, validated: boolean, contested: boolean }] }]` over the visible records (RFC-33 R2) — categories in dictionary order, traits by key, only traits with at least one visible record. `levels` is set for categorical traits: every level among the harmonised records, with no cap, `count` descending then key; `validationCount` counts the distinct actors who validated at least one record of the level, and `contested` follows R14. `numeric` is set for quantitative traits, over the harmonised records whose `unit_status` is not `needs_unit_check` (a record with `unit_missing` still counts, since the standard unit is assumed) — null when none qualify. The qualifying records are grouped by effective unit, the record's `unit` or, when it has none, the trait's standard unit (R1), and `numeric` holds one entry per unit, `count` descending then `unit` ascending (a null unit last); numbers in different units are never averaged together, since the colour traits' scales are not interconvertible. Within one entry, `single` is the mean of the single value over the qualifying records that hold it, since each one is a measurement; each of `mean`, `median`, `min` and `max` is the mean of that field over the distinct (`primary_reference_id`, `secondary_reference_id`, value) among the entry's qualifying records that hold it, so a study's summary repeated on each of its measurement rows counts once (two summary-only records from the same references with the same value also count once, an accepted limit); an entry is `null` when no qualifying record holds its field — the fields are never pooled, so a bound is never averaged with a central value (R15) — `sd_value`, `se_value` and `n` never enter these figures, and `count` is the number of the entry's qualifying records, those holding at least one of the five fields. `validated` follows R11; `contested` is true when a level or record of the trait is contested (R14). Unknown species answers 404 `SPECIES_NOT_FOUND`.
- **R11** No accepted value. The platform stores every claim and never picks one. There is no accepted value, no route that sets one and no permission that decides one (`accepted_values` and `accepted.manage` are removed by plan 13e; RFC-65 R6 is retired). A species × trait is *validated* when at least one of its visible records (RFC-33 R2) has a `confirm` annotation (RFC-65 R3). Every count that counted accepted pairs counts validated pairs instead (RFC-62 R7, RFC-69 R5, R7, RFC-72 R1, RFC-52 R1).
- **R12** Record ID. `record_code` is `text not null unique`. An imported record takes the file's `ID` column (RFC-64 R2, R7). When a row is split into several records (RFC-64 R6), each part gets the ID plus a letter suffix in part order (`EB_1a`, `EB_1b`, …; after `z`, `aa`, `ab`, …). A record created on the platform (RFC-65 R1, R9, RFC-70 R3) takes `'TR_' || nextval('record_code_tr_seq')`; a form that creates several levels takes one sequence number and suffixes it the same way (`TR_7a`, `TR_7b`, …). A single-level entry keeps the bare code. Stored codes match `^(EB|TR)_[0-9]+([a-z]+)?$`. Gaps in the `TR_` sequence are accepted. A `TR_` code never changes and is never reused (R4); an `EB_` code names its source row and is carried by the replacing record of a preserving replace (RFC-64 R15).
- **R13** A withdrawn record leaves the dataset. A record with a `withdraw` annotation (RFC-65 R4) is visible to no viewer (RFC-33 R2): it disappears from lists, counts, trait cards, coverage, filters, queues, contributions and the export, and no strikethrough stays behind. It stays in `trait_records` for audit only. The stored counters — `species_trait_coverage` and `species.trait_count` (RFC-69 R2), a reference's `primary_count` and `secondary_count` (RFC-61 R4) and `reference_traits.record_count` (RFC-61 R9) — decrement in the transaction that inserts the `withdraw` annotation, so each counts the records that are not withdrawn.
- **R14** Contested. A *contest* is an entry of RFC-70 with `intent = 'contest'`. A categorical contest states which levels are correct. Against E, the active levels with a visible record of the species and trait when it is submitted, and S, the levels it gives: the levels of S ∩ E become its author's validations (RFC-70 R3), the levels of S \ E become new records with `intent = 'contest'` and no `responds_to_record_id`, and the levels of E \ S are the levels it *contests* (`contestedLevelIds`, RFC-70 R1). That set is fixed at submission: a level that first gains a visible record later is not contested by it. Among {`red`, `blue`, `yellow`}, a contest giving only `red` validates `red` and contests `blue` and `yellow`; one giving only `green` creates a `green` record and contests all three. A categorical contest is stored with its author, its time and the levels it contests whether or not it creates a record: one row of `contests`, `contest_levels` naming the levels it contests and `contest_records` the records it created; `contest_events` holds its `resolve` and — for a contest that created none — its `withdraw`. A quantitative contest is one record with `intent = 'contest'` that responds to the one record it contests. A contest is *withdrawn* once every record it created is withdrawn, or through RFC-65 R16 (the only way for a contest that created none); it is *resolved* once it carries Keep both (RFC-65 R15, R16). A contest is *standing* while it is neither withdrawn nor resolved and what it contests still has a visible record: at least one level it names, or, for a quantitative contest, the record it responds to. A level emptied by Withdraw level that later gets a visible record again is contested again by a contest that is still standing. A level is *contested* while a standing contest names it and the level still has a visible record; a quantitative record is contested while it is visible and a standing contest responds to it. A species × trait is contested when one of its levels or records is. Contested is the only status badge the platform shows (R6's other states are filter values and counts), and every viewer sees it. It is derived from contests alone, never from `dispute` annotations.
- **R15** Quantitative value. Each quantity has its own field, and the platform never derives one from another: `numeric_value` is the *single* value, and `mean_value`, `median_value`, `min_value`, `max_value`, `sd_value` and `se_value` are nullable `numeric`, and `n` is a nullable `integer`. No label says what a field is; the field is the statement, and the user decides how to combine them. Database checks enforce `min_value <= max_value` when both are present, `sd_value >= 0`, `se_value >= 0` and `n >= 1`. A manual quantitative record has at least one of single, min, max and mean (RFC-65 R1); manual entry never sets `median_value` or `se_value` — both are import-only (RFC-64 R6). The import fills the single, mean or median value from the row's value and its statistic, and the range, the spread and the sample size from their columns (RFC-64 R6); it never computes a midpoint or converts `se` to `sd`. The `value_text` of a quantitative record is the single value as PostgreSQL prints `numeric` when it is the only field present. Otherwise it lists the present fields as `<name>=<value>`, joined by `;` in the order `single`, `min`, `max`, `mean`, `median`, `sd`, `se`, `n` (`min=2;max=5;se=0.3;n=12`, `mean=5`), so two records that differ in any field never collide under R3. `sd_value` and `se_value` are spreads and are never summarised as if they were values of the trait; `min_value` and `max_value` are the bounds of one observation and are never averaged into a mean (R10; RFC-62 R7, R8).
- **R16** Several references. A manual record's first reference is `primary_reference_id`. Its further references are rows of `record_references(record_id uuid references trait_records, reference_id uuid references bibliographic_references, primary key (record_id, reference_id))`, written in the record's transaction and never changed. Imported records have no `record_references` rows. Screens and the export list a record's references primary first, joined by `; `. On a record item the further references follow the primary one in `citation_key` order; they are never personal observations (RFC-61 R7). A `record_references` row counts as a primary usage of its reference (RFC-61 R4, R9).
