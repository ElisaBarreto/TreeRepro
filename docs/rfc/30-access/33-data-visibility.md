# RFC-33 — Data visibility

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | accepted |
| Category | access control |
| Supersedes | — |

## Context

Some species and traits are not ready for the contributor community: traits still being defined, species outside the current phase or with unverified taxonomy. Contributors are recruited through field plots (RFC-67) and may be confined to them. The catalog therefore has row-level visibility, decided by the API for every read and write (RFC-02 R1), never by the web app.

## Rules

- **R1** Viewer. Every dataset read and write is evaluated for a *viewer*: the session user, their effective permissions (RFC-32 R1) and their plot settings (RFC-67). `Visibility` is the value `{ inactive: boolean, plotIds: string[] | null, platform }`: `inactive` is true when the viewer holds `dataset.read_inactive`; `plotIds` is the viewer's assigned plots when `restrict_to_assigned_plots` is true, else `null` (no plot restriction); `platform` is the platform of the request (`c.var.platform`, RFC-14 R3). It is computed once per request by `visibilityOf(ctx, c)` and passed to every service call.
- **R2** A species is *visible* to a viewer when (`species.active` or `visibility.inactive`) and (`visibility.plotIds` is null or the species belongs to one of those plots) and it is in the platform's scope: `visibility.platform = 'plantrepro'` or (`plant_type = 'tree'` and `plant_type_status = 'reviewed'`) (RFC-14 R9, RFC-60 R1). TreeRepro therefore shows only reviewed trees, and PlantRepro every species; a record, contest or annotation on a species outside the scope is invisible like the species. No other rule differs between the platforms (RFC-14 R2). A trait is visible when `traits.active` or `visibility.inactive`. A level is visible when `trait_levels.active` or `visibility.inactive`. A record is visible when its species, its trait and — for a categorical record — its level are visible, it is not withdrawn (RFC-63 R13), it counts as evidence (`counts_as_evidence`, RFC-63 R17: a copy of evidence another dataset already gives is kept but never read), and its `harmonisation` is `harmonised` or the viewer holds `records.review` (pending records, unknown levels and the like are reviewer work). The unresolved-taxon flag (RFC-60 R6) is reviewer-only data too.
- **R3** Lists omit invisible rows: `GET /api/species`; `GET /api/genera` and `GET /api/families` (a genus or family with no visible species is omitted; one with no species at all stays listed for a viewer who holds `dataset.read_inactive` and has no plot restriction, so a catalog editor can fill a genus or family just created, while one whose species are all outside the platform's scope (R2) is omitted for that viewer too); `GET /api/traits` (invisible traits and levels omitted; an unrestricted viewer still receives everything with `active: false`); `GET /api/species/:id/traits`; `GET /api/records`; `GET /api/records/pending/traits`; `GET /api/records/pending`; `GET /api/records/disputed`. Counters are unaffected, whether stored or computed per request: a reference's `primaryCount`, `secondaryCount` and `recordCount` (RFC-61 R4) and a species' `traitCount` and `recordCount` (RFC-60 R7, RFC-69 R1) count every record that is not withdrawn (RFC-63 R13) and counts as evidence (RFC-63 R17) for every viewer, so the visible parts beside them need not sum to them. The rule covers every route that returns dataset rows in any form — JSON, CSV, e-mail, cached aggregate — and this list is exhaustive: a new such route is added here (or to the exceptions below) before it is merged, so the review catches the omission. Beyond the routes above: `GET /api/species/:id` (RFC-60); `GET /api/traits/:id` and `GET /api/traits/:id/species` (RFC-62 R7, R8); `GET /api/references` and `GET /api/references/:id` (RFC-61); `GET /api/records/:id` (RFC-63); `GET /api/plots/:id/species` (RFC-67); `GET /api/coverage` and `GET /api/coverage/top` (RFC-69); `GET /api/me/contributions` and `GET /api/admin/users/:id/contributions` (RFC-71); `GET /api/me/dashboard` (RFC-72); `GET /api/export/dataset.zip` (RFC-66 R2). `GET /api/curation/plant-types` (RFC-65 R20) applies R2 without its platform condition (R11). Exceptions, unrestricted by design and named as such in their RFCs: the global counters above and the trait dictionary's species counts (RFC-62 R5, R7), which drop the plot dimension but keep the platform's scope; `GET /api/me/contributions/summary` and `GET /api/admin/users/:id/contributions/summary` (RFC-71 R4, R5); the daily digest e-mail (RFC-74 R3); the platform health payload (RFC-52).
- **R4** Detail routes answer 404 for an invisible row with the resource's code: `SPECIES_NOT_FOUND`, `TRAIT_NOT_FOUND`, `RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. The existence of an invisible row is never disclosed.
- **R5** Writes: `POST /api/records` and `POST /api/records/pending/map` on an invisible species, trait or level answer the same 404 / 400 as for an unknown one; `POST /api/records/:id/annotations` on an invisible record answers 404, and the level actions of RFC-65 R13 and R14 answer for an invisible species, trait or level what they answer for an unknown one; the contest actions of RFC-65 R16 answer 404 `RECORD_NOT_FOUND` for a contest on an invisible species, trait or named level. Catalog writes (`taxa.manage`, `traits.manage`, `references.manage`) take the same visibility; their holders are unrestricted by role design and the services do not assume it. A write naming a species outside the platform's scope (R2) answers 404 `SPECIES_NOT_FOUND` like an unknown one, whatever else the actor may see: `PATCH /api/species/:id`, `POST /api/species/:id/names`, `POST /api/plots/:id/species` and `DELETE /api/plots/:id/species/:speciesId` (RFC-67 R5) check the platform condition alone; `POST /api/species/:id/plant-type` (RFC-65 R19) and every record, annotation, level and contest write check it through R2. A TreeRepro user therefore cannot write to a non-tree species through the API either. The plant-type confirmation of RFC-65 R20 is the one exception (R11). `POST /api/records/pending/map` maps only the pending rows of visible species; the trait and its levels are checked the same way.
- **R6** Species scope (RFC-67): `GET /api/species` accepts `scope=plots|all` and `plotId=`. Default: `plots` when the viewer has at least one assigned plot and does not hold `dataset.read_inactive` (managers and admins have no plot-scoped home view), else `all`. `scope=plots` restricts the list to the species of the viewer's plots; `scope=all` lists every visible species; `plotId` restricts to one plot and ignores `scope`. A plot-bound viewer asking `scope=all`, or `plotId` of a plot they are not assigned to, answers 403 `PERMISSION_DENIED`. An unknown `plotId` answers 404 `PLOT_NOT_FOUND`.
- **R7** Representation: the species list item and detail carry `active: boolean` (RFC-60 R6, R7); the trait entry already carries `active`. A restricted viewer only ever receives `true`.
- **R8** `GET /api/auth/me` carries `scope: { plots: [{ id, code, name }], restricted: boolean }` (RFC-22 R10) so the web app can render the plot toggle and default the species list; presentation only (RFC-13 R3).
- **R9** Every service that reads species, traits, levels or records, and every route that returns dataset rows in any form (R3) — JSON, CSV, e-mail, cached aggregate — has an integration test with two viewers — one restricted, one unrestricted — over the same fixture (an inactive species with a record on an active trait; an active species with a record on an inactive trait; an active species with a record on an inactive level of an active trait) asserting omission versus presence. A route listed among the R3 exceptions has the test the other way round: both viewers receive the same rows. The platform scope (R2) has a fixture of its own — a reviewed tree, a reviewed `not_tree` and a proposed tree — read on both hosts: on TreeRepro the last two are absent from every list, detail, count, coverage figure, contribution and export, and a write to them answers 404; on PlantRepro all three are present.
- **R10** Meta-test. A test enumerates every registered route whose guard names one of the permissions that read the dataset — `dataset.read`, `dataset.export`, `coverage.read`, `records.review`, `contributions.read` (`VISIBILITY_GUARDED_PERMISSIONS`, `apps/api/src/http/visibility-routes.ts`) — calls each as an unrestricted viewer over a fixture that lets it succeed, and asserts that the handler resolved the viewer's visibility (R1: `visibilityOf` records the value on the request context), unless the route is on `VISIBILITY_EXEMPT_ROUTES` beside that constant. The exempt list names the routes behind those permissions that return no dataset rows, each citing its rule: `GET /api/me/contributions/summary` and `GET /api/admin/users/:id/contributions/summary` (RFC-71 R4, R5), `GET /api/plots` and `GET /api/plots/:id` (RFC-67 R3: plots are the recruitment structure, not scientific data). The test also fails on an exempt route that does not exist or that resolves visibility, so the list stays exact. R3 stays the human-readable list; this test is its executable form: a route behind one of those permissions that neither takes a `Visibility` nor sits on the exempt list fails the build.

- **R11** Bypasses. Every reader that does not take the request's viewer is listed here with the platform it uses and why:
  - `UNRESTRICTED` (`apps/api/src/access/visibility.ts`) is every species of both platforms, inactive ones included: the CLI commands (`verify:summaries` compares every species of the owner's files, RFC-77), and the detail a catalog write answers after it has passed its own platform check (R5) — so a species created on TreeRepro by a creator without `records.review`, which starts `proposed` (RFC-65 R18) and is hidden there, is still echoed to its creator — and the reference detail a reference write answers (references are shared by both platforms; their trait list is filtered by trait visibility only).
  - The platform health payload (RFC-52) is one page for the whole deployment: its grid is the active catalog of both platforms, its queue counters unrestricted.
  - The digest (RFC-74 R3) is computed for the platform it is sent for: its queue counts and its contest list take that platform's scope; the counts over the window stay platform-wide numbers that name no species.
  - The region snapshot (RFC-78 R7–R12) is computed with TreeRepro's scope until it is computed per platform (plan 15f), so TreeRepro's maps never count a non-tree.
  - The stored counters of R3 — a reference's `primaryCount`, `secondaryCount` and `recordCount`, a species' `traitCount` — and the dashboard's reference counts (RFC-72 R1) belong to the shared catalog (RFC-61) and count every record of both platforms.
  - A plot's `speciesCount` in `GET /api/plots` and `GET /api/plots/:id` (RFC-67 R3) counts every species the plot holds on both platforms: plots are the recruitment structure, not scientific data (R10), and the count names no species. The plot's species list (`GET /api/plots/:id/species`) takes the viewer's visibility, so on TreeRepro it may list fewer species than the count.
  - Species proposals (RFC-75) are names, not dataset rows: the proposal queues and their counts are shared by both platforms, as are a decided proposal's `species` reference, which on TreeRepro may name a species whose page answers 404. Only the name pre-checks take the platform's scope (RFC-60 R13, RFC-75 R2).
  - The plant-type review queue (RFC-65 R20): its list, its count (RFC-72 R1, RFC-74 R3) and its confirmation apply R2 without the platform condition to the species whose status is `proposed`, for `records.review` holders only, so a manager of either platform can review any proposed type (spec §3). They show what the queue needs and no more — name, genus, family, proposer, stated type and the proposal note — and the species page stays 404 on TreeRepro until its type is confirmed as `tree`.
